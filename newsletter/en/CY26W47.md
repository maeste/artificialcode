## RSI leaves the labs: the AI that accelerates AI moves into industry

Lately we keep hearing about recursive self-improvement (RSI), that is, the ability of models, and more generally, to improve themselves during their life cycle. I have talked about it many times myself, with different definitions or nuances, mentioning for example self-evolving harnesses, which we came across again just last week among the themes of the Technology Radar. This week I dedicate the deep dive to it, because it is important to observe that this technique is leaving pure research labs: in recent months we have had plenty of research papers on recursive self-improvement and self-evolving harnesses, but precisely, as I was saying, it is now leaving research labs to land in industry.

And the effects are already very concrete, showing an impressive acceleration in the time it takes to consolidate new discoveries. The document that puts numbers on this feeling is the [independent assessment METR conducted with high access inside Anthropic](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-05-anthropic-ai-rd-acceleration-assessment/) between August and October: capabilities on well-specified tasks are progressing at about twice the speed of the previous year, 1.9x on the AECI index with an interval of 1.5-2.8, and a large part of the acceleration, perhaps 80%, is attributable to AI doing R&D. METR also points out that the jump cannot be explained by training compute scale alone. We are talking about two years of progress in one, with personnel reporting up to 12x uplift in their daily work.

You see this very clearly in pure research proper, that is, on verifiable research subjects, such as mathematics, other sciences, or programming itself. But the most interesting part is another one: this technique is being applied more and more to AI research itself, doing recursive self-improvement both on models, and here I mean model architecture as well as training techniques, and on self-evolving AI. The two paths are exactly the ones [Tom Mann](https://x.com/TomasMann1878/status/2119346730458038678) describes in his retrospective of his first year as a research engineer on Meta's RSI team: updating the foundation model, or improving the scaffolding around it, made of prompts, tools and memory. Teaching research agents, as he writes, to solve the world's hardest problems with tokens and compute. And his first paper, Research Preference Models, serves exactly this purpose: selecting promising experiments to try at low cost.

It is a very important trend, which by moving into industry and, slowly but ever more concretely, into the enterprise world too, risks changing the rules of the game once again. And here the readings get serious. [Ajeya Cotra](https://www.planned-obsolescence.org/p/ai-is-dramatically-accelerating-ai) estimates that doubling progress would require a 4-9x acceleration of labor, plausibly around 10x today, and observes that the dramatic acceleration threshold foreseen by Anthropic's RSP is now behind us: with no bottlenecks, the naive extrapolation goes a very long way in a very short time. She calls it the most important thing that has happened in AI risk in years. [Zvi Mowshowitz](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/ai-r-and-d-is-accelerating-towards) notes that the pace is already months ahead of the AI 2027 scenario timeline, and that the situation is grim, but it is not a surprise. With due caveats: METR itself signals wide uncertainty in both directions, Toby Ord sees a one-time jump in the Mythos pretrain rather than a slope change, and Anthropic redacted most of the most informative evidence, in practice asking us for an act of trust.

I invite you, loyal readers of this newsletter, not to underestimate it, and to take the time to read the articles I propose and to dig deeper into the topic.

### 🤖 Agentic AI

Three important pieces of news in the agents world, because they touch three different aspects that bring them closer to the enterprise world. On one hand, the ever stronger need for self-hosted agents, for security and practical reasons. On the other, the importance of memory, and finally we start seeing new ways of interacting with these agents that are not just conversation and natural language.

#### [Operate Claude Managed Agents inside your security perimeter](https://claude.com/resources/articles/self-hosted-claude-managed-agents)
*Private beta: the whole service for autonomous agents runs in the customer's cloud, sessions in their database, models via Anthropic, Bedrock or internal gateways. Credentials outside the sandbox, exportable sessions.*

#### [A2UI 1.0: Build delightful, fast, reliable, agentic experiences](https://developers.googleblog.com/a2ui-10-build-delightful-fast-reliable-agentic-experiences/)
*Stable version of the open protocol for generative UI: agents answer with interactive screens rendered by each app's native components; sensitive values never reach the model.*

#### [The components of agent memory](https://www.anthropic.com/research/components-of-agent-memory)
*Claude's memory hierarchy (transcripts, notes, wikis, injected context) with dreaming, the offline reflection that corrects and consolidates. Wikis raise accuracy from 53% to 61% on a 420M token corpus.*

### 🔬 AI and science

This is a trend too: AI accelerating science, especially in the biological world. DeepMind has always led the way on these things, and confirms it with AlphaGenome.

#### [AlphaGenome 2: Deciphering how cells read DNA to better understand disease](https://deepmind.google/blog/alphagenome-2-deciphering-how-cells-read-dna-to-better-understand-disease/)
*A conditional model to predict the effects of genetic variants per cell type and state, conditionable on your own single-cell data. Almost double the causal variant recall compared to the previous version.*
