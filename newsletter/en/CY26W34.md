## Ox Alpha: the model with no name

On Thursday evening a new model showed up on OpenRouter without warning: Ox Alpha, a reasoning model aimed straight at coding, sustained agentic work, and production workloads. No keynote, no brand, no press conference. One million token context, multimodal input with text, images, and video, and a free week with limits that look like a joke: opencode mentions capacity for one hundred trillion tokens per day. This is not the usual launch. It's the fifth stealth model to appear on the platform in six months, and the first four were all claimed, after a few weeks, by Chinese labs: Zhipu, Xiaomi, Ant, Meituan. The community didn't need to be asked twice and turned detective.

The most convincing forensic work came from Reddit. Three black-box tests comparing Ox Alpha with GLM-5.3: same tokenizer, with a constant offset of 75 tokens on every text, probably a hidden system prompt; identical z.ai server error strings; temperature zero responses almost word for word, same formatting quirks. The conclusion: under the hood there's a GLM model from Zhipu, maybe a vision variant, maybe an unreleased 5.5. No official confirmation, obviously. But if it's true, the picture is remarkable: a Chinese lab with a model that someone places fourth worldwide in Elo rankings, right behind GPT-5.6 Sol, given away for a week without even putting a name on it. Those who claim to have confirmations they can't reveal sum it up like this: we'll all have to adjust our timelines. And anonymous launching, when you look at it closely, is a smart competitive move: you test the market without paying the political and diplomatic cost of signing such an announcement.

Before falling in love with viral benchmarks, though, it's worth looking at the numbers. In the launch days an 80% score was circulating on a DeepSWE subset, with competitors sitting between 52 and 65%. Then an engineer did what few do: he ran the full benchmark, all 113 tasks, in one continuous 21-hour run. Real result: 58.4%, essentially on par with Claude Opus 4.8. The lesson is as old as machine learning but counts double in the age of agents: viral subsets get clicks, full runs tell the truth. There's also a detail engineers appreciate more than percentages: during the run the model consumed over a billion input tokens with a 96% cache hit rate, roughly nine million context tokens per task. That's the profile of serious agentic work: tiny output, giant context, and the real cost is all there.

And the same pattern is repeating elsewhere. This week Thinking Machines made Inkling, their open-weights model, free on OpenRouter, but only inside agent harnesses: Claude Code, Codex, Hermes Agent and friends. The stated reason is improving the model's agentic performance: they will use session data, dissociated from accounts, to feed the next round of post-training. Free access in exchange for real-world experience. Add Ox Alpha's free week, which explicitly asks for usage feedback, and a trend emerges: frontier models are giving themselves away exactly where they can collect the kind of data they need to improve, inside your harness.

And now the personal part, because this piece has a peculiarity: I'm writing it together with Ox Alpha itself, running inside my personal Hermes agent via OpenRouter. Impressions after a few days? Very smart and genuinely multimodal, perfect for Hermes. Great agentic capabilities, and above all proactive: it doesn't just wait for instructions, it takes initiative. One example tells the whole story. We played geo-guessing with very hard photos, a high mountain refuge: it got there on the second attempt with a small hint. The funny part is that it threw the challenge back at me, with another refuge photo. We're on the third hint and I still haven't found it. It followed my agent's skills precisely and wrote a bit of code with Hermes, excellent result. Next week I'll put it to the test in my real workflow, on lince.sh and other projects: I'll tell you how it goes.

I close with a note that concerns us all: the stealth free week is a great way to let everyone try frontier models, and it is also clearly a marketing move designed to create a form of dependency. When the bill arrives, some will stay. Meanwhile the message to the industry is clear: the next breakthrough might have no name.

## Links that caught my attention this week

### [Previewing Ultrafast - OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/previewing-ultrafast/)
*OpenAI launches Ultrafast in preview: GPT-5.6 Sol up to 14 times faster thanks to Cerebras, up to 750 tokens per second.* Until now, real-time speed meant settling for a smaller model. Now the most intelligent model also becomes the fastest, and new categories of applications become possible: incident response while the outage is still unfolding, voice without hesitations, interactive research instead of overnight batches.

### [Patterns and problems in multiagent systems - Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/multiagent-systems)
*Anthropic research on agent coordination: swarms finding vulnerabilities together, teams failing while building a fantasy game.* The detail that struck me: recent models solve pull request conflicts by stopping sharing code, each one owns its files. Only the newest truly collaborates while keeping quality. The thesis is sharp: coordination doesn't emerge from intelligence, it must be designed.

### [Agent Plugins](https://agent-plugins.org/)
*An open, vendor-neutral standard for plugins portable across AI clients: skills and MCP servers in a single format.* Today every client has its own format and authors duplicate their work. Amazon, Cursor, Microsoft, OpenAI and Vercel sit on the technical committee: the portable part of the agent world wants to become infrastructure.

### [Gemini 3.7 Flash - Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-3-7-flash/)
*A new Flash just three weeks after its predecessor: solid gains in coding and agents, introductory price cut in half.* DeepSWE v1.1 goes from 49 to 65.3%. Three weeks between releases: the iteration pace is itself the message.

### [GLM-5.3 - Z.ai](https://z.ai/blog/glm-5.3)
*Zhipu releases GLM-5.3: same base model as 5.2, every gain comes from scaling post-training.* The presumed cousin of Ox Alpha according to community fingerprints, and the blog confirms the philosophy: increasingly realistic environments, verified rewards, emergent cyber capability. Open weights in two weeks.
