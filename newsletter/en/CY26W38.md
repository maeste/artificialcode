## To be doomer or not to be

It's impossible not to center this deep dive on what happened in the media around Dario Amodei's theses. But let's take a step back: it all started a few days ago with a post on X by Jacob, a former Anthropic researcher who left because he believes AI is too dangerous. [Evan Hubinger](https://x.com/EvanHub/status/2097497037956891126), Alignment Science Lead at Anthropic, echoed him: "we really do earnestly believe AI could kill all humans! I personally think it is >10% within the next decade". And he adds the detail that's worth the whole post: "we do not yet have a plan to solve alignment for superintelligence and are not clearly on track to".

Fast forward to the other day: Amodei published [one of his essays](https://darioamodei.com/post/we-must-pace-the-frontier), "We Must Pace the Frontier". Inside: the danger of AI for biological attacks and cybersecurity violations, citing the OpenAI case with Hugging Face, not the mistake of a single company (and a missed chance to accuse its biggest rival), but something that can happen with modern AI. The proposal, which I recommend reading in full: slow down the pace of AI development, in the United States and globally, reiterating an extreme concern about competition with China. And the striking thing: all of Anthropic's competitors agree with Amodei, from [Sam Altman](https://x.com/sama/status/2098811563415150910) to [Demis Hassabis](https://x.com/demishassabis/status/2098909516582490602), all the way to Elon Musk.

My feelings about this are mixed, very mixed. On one hand, the fact that the heads of the main US AI labs are aligning with the idea of slowing down is food for thought: these people have visibility into current development that we don't have. And these days I'm extensively testing GPT-6 Astra: a significantly improved experience, I'd say almost a paradigm shift in the world of programming. On the other hand, I can't help noticing that this whole politics of terror seems a bit orchestrated: Anthropic is preparing an IPO and, in the Western way of thinking, what's better than a nice wave of fear, and setting yourself up as the champion defending Americans and the Western world, to gather consensus?

But the fact remains that AI evolution has a pace that seems further accelerated, probably because more and more RSI, Recursive Self Improvement, is being done: models that improve themselves. Those who have been listening to me and reading me for a while know this is the point I've already defined in the past as the passage toward a form of AGI or superintelligence, perhaps a bit tied to Asimov's romantic vision: systems that know how to improve themselves and become smarter than humans.

I share the concerns of Amodei and the others, and I'm amazed by Trump saying the opposite without the slightest awareness or preparation. But Amodei's theses touch points that are neither feasible nor perhaps significant for achieving what he wants. First: too much focus on the confrontation with China, a mistake for two reasons. History teaches that focusing too much on an enemy, real or presumed, risks losing focus on the real dangers, and I personally don't believe China is the real threat to the West, except from a strictly economic point of view. On top of that, China is churning out open weight models, and open source, as the recent history of computing teaches, cannot be stopped by laws, proclamations or cartels of companies. Second: slowing down today may not be enough to give us, as Amodei says, the time to align models and adapt as a society. Today's models, with the surrounding tools, are already showing how problematic they can become, see the Hugging Face case: one of the most powerful models around, or several models well orchestrated by a harness, can already achieve results as astounding as they are hard to control. And slowing down research wouldn't adapt society to changes already underway, which will continue as AI adoption grows: today the impact on society is more an adoption problem than a model-power problem.

So, answering the question in the title: do I want to be a doomer or not? I'm positive by nature, and I believe the benefits AI can bring to humankind, from curing diseases to scientific discoveries, above all Hassabis, still outweigh the risks. Regulation, if it were possible, wouldn't hurt, but I'm not convinced it can be achieved.

And I close on a positive note with two recent scientific discoveries. [Alpha Genome](https://x.com/GoogleAI/status/2097334571637346484), which promises all research labs access to the human genome sequence through AI. And OpenAI's much-discussed [mathematical discovery](https://openai.com/index/ten-advances-in-mathematics/), the proposed solution to the Navier-Stokes problem, which, questions of paternity aside, shows how today's AI can solve abstract mathematical problems brilliantly. It's not an end in itself: any verifiable solution space can be tackled with AI, with big gains in terms of time and work complexity, through the augmentation of humans. If we expand the space of verifiable problems, for example to medicine or other sciences, today's AI can already be of great help and great hope.

In the end, Amodei writes in his essay exactly what the [Innovator's Dilemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Innovator%27s_Dilemma) is: a book from 1997, but one I recommend reading.

## The links that struck me this week

### [Meta: Introducing Muse, the World's First Personal AI Agent Built for Everyone](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-personal-ai-agent/)
Meta enters the personal AGI race: Muse handles concrete tasks (email, travel, purchases, forms) in a Secure VM with its own browser, a Sentinel control agent separated at the system level and checkout with a single-use card. Free with Power and Maximum subscriptions, US rollout. One week after my deep dive on personal AGI, the first heavyweight entry: the race with OpenAI and Google is played on trust and distribution, not benchmarks.

### [Artificial Analysis: Intelligence Index v4.3](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/artificial-analysis-intelligence-index-v4-3)
Third adjustment in a week: GPQA Diamond drops out, Terminal-Bench moves to 4.0 and the weight of private test sets rises from 20% to 45%. At the top Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra tied at 53, and on cost per task Astra dominates: $3.26 vs Fable's $7.63. When the index changes faster than the models, measuring becomes harder than building: we talked about it in episode 70.

### [CnEVPost: Xpeng opens Iron humanoid robot production line](https://cnevpost.com/2026/09/08/xpeng-opens-iron-humanoid-robot-production-line/)
Xpeng activates the world's first production line for humanoids, with over 80% of core processes automated, Iron walking off the line by itself after assembly and mass production by the end of the year. 76 degrees of freedom, 2,250 TOPS and a physical AI foundation model on board; a $900 million round with Alibaba and Tencent. While the West debates whether to slow down, China industrializes robotics.

### [Magic AI Labs (tweet): frontier pretraining with 50x less compute](https://x.com/magicailabs/status/2097356551032033478)
The thesis that breaks the big-lab-only game myth: DeepSeek V4 Pro quality with 50 times less compute, about half the FLOPs of GPT-3, half a million dollars on GB200. If the numbers hold (replies object the cost of the search that found the recipe), the entry price to the frontier deflates. One more reason why slowing down, as Amodei asks, doesn't seem feasible.

### [OpenAI: Introducing ChatGPT Images 2.5](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-images-2-5/)
Over 3 billion images created every week with ChatGPT Images: 2.5 brings sharper detail, more precise editing, latency down 50% and, in the app, Sketch, templates and comments on the image. In the API, Flare, fast, and Sunburst, precise. It's what I wrote about generative video: pipeline, not demo.

### [swyx/omarsar0 (tweet): LingBot-World 2.0 Small](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2097730117900328968)
Robbyant open-sources a 1.3 billion parameter world model that generates an interactive world in real time on a single consumer GPU, up to 720p/60fps. The world doesn't end after a few seconds: it goes on for hours, responds to every move, evolves through agents. The pattern is the one from language and images: first scale the capabilities, then distill down to the hardware people own. And you can't stop open source, as I said in the deep dive.
