## Personal AGI: the war is over the harness, not the model

This week everyone is talking about [GPT-6 Astra](https://openai.com/index/safety-overview-gpt-6-astra/). We covered it at length on the podcast: 99.9% on ARC-AGI-3, the [announcement](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2095595741528125780) that racked up hundreds of thousands of likes in three days, and [Chollet's take](https://x.com/fchollet/status/2095598451115614371) calling it a step-function change, with the model building its own symbolic language on the fly to reason through the games. On first contact it is, in some ways, incredible. And yet the news that struck me most came from the same OpenAI through a different door: the [North Stars](https://openai.com/index/an-alien-mind/), the public strategic plan written by chief scientist Jakub Pachocki. Because inside it there's a word that describes the real contest of this moment: personal AGI.

OpenAI is the only one that has put it in black and white. Three goals: the [automated AI researcher](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/03/20/1134438/openai-is-throwing-everything-into-building-a-fully-automated-researcher/), economic acceleration, and a "personal AGI for every person on Earth". No formal definition, of course. The operational version is in the [Personal AGI team job posting](https://openai.com/careers/research-engineerresearch-scientist-personal-agi-north-stars-san-francisco/): "evolve ChatGPT from a chatbot to an infinitely capable and personalized superassistant". [Brockman](https://gln75.com/en/blog/brockman-openai-superapp-path-agi) puts numbers on it: AGI at "70-80%", the definition no longer matters, the floor is rising too fast. And [the retweet](https://x.com/gdb/status/2093065379145019902) that half the internet ran with: ChatGPT Work booking a haircut on its own, "chatgpt is increasingly becoming your personal AGI". Then February: [Peter Steinberger, the creator of OpenClaw](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/15/openclaw-creator-peter-steinberger-joins-openai/), the most widely used open harness in the world, joins OpenAI to lead personal AI agents. Read that carefully: the lab with the most coveted models on the planet brought in-house the person who knows how to build execution, not weights.

But OpenAI's reading keeps the model at the center: personal AGI is ChatGPT, a model, wrapped in their harness in their cloud. I see it differently, and I'm not alone. Nous Research has never used that word for [Hermes](https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/docs/), and it's no accident: their agent is self-improving, model-agnostic across twenty providers, skills are created from experience, the model is a configuration variable. The harness is the product.

And the harness now has its theory. The paper ["Stop Comparing LLM Agents Without Disclosing the Harness"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23950) formalizes the Binding Constraint Thesis: on long-horizon tasks, performance variance induced by the harness exceeds that induced by the model, by a factor of seven in their experiment. ["The Harness Effect"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06906) runs the clean experiment: same six models, same tasks, only the orchestration changes. Cost per task down 41%, time down 44%, tokens down 38%.

My thesis is simpler than any paper: personal AGI will be an engineering solution, not a model one. A harness orchestrating multiple agents, each with the right model for the job: a local one for privacy, a cloud one for capability, a cheap one for batch work. Not a supermodel at the center, a well-conducted orchestra. Exactly how my own setup works, where my personal agent runs on my server, with the model I choose, and this week I had it test the sandbox that protects the others.

Personal AGI means one thing only: whoever owns the harness owns the agent. The rest is marketing.

## The links that struck me this week

### [Anthropic: Introducing Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Mythos 5.1](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-1)

Anthropic releases two versions of the same model with different safeguard levels. Fable 5.1 is GA on API, AWS, GCP and Azure: 55.8% on Terminal-Bench 4.0, cache reads at $0.25/M (-75%), safeguards with a massively reduced false-positive rate. Mythos 5.1, the more permissive version, stays gated to verified US organizations (CVP and LSVP). One model, many products: safeguard segmentation becomes a product variable.

### [Google: Introducing Gemini 3.8 Flash and 3.8 Flash Cyber](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/3-8-flash-and-3-8-flash-cyber/)

Third Flash in six weeks. The thesis: 3.8 Flash "works harder", more reasoning steps and more tool calls at the same price and speed as 3.7. Introductory pricing of $0.75/$3.75 per million tokens expiring December 31, 2026, then double: the Flash war shifts to unit economics. Alongside it, Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber specialized in patching (47.2% pass@1 on CWE-Bench), gated access via the Fairwind Program.

### [Google: Gemini Omni 1.1 Flash lets you build with more control](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/build-with-gemini-omni-1-1-flash/)

Google brings the Omni video model to production-ready 1.1: analysis of up to 10 seconds of previous footage for visual coherence, scene extension up to 40 cumulative seconds, keyframe control for transitions and loops, video reference input for character consistency. 360p draft previews cost a third of 720p. Runway, Figma and Adobe among the cited customers: generative video becomes a pipeline, not a demo.

### [NVIDIA: NVIDIA to Acquire Hugging Face](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-to-acquire-hugging-face/)

NVIDIA acquires Hugging Face for $12,930,300,000, the figure written with cent-level precision. In exchange: 18M+ developers, 3M+ models, 500K datasets. The guarantees in Jensen Huang's post: the platform stays open, NVIDIA compute will not be required to build or deploy, multi-cloud support and the 🤗 brand maintained. The neutral hub of open weights has an owner: the neutrality promise should be read as the buyer's positioning.

### [Rachel Laycock, rachels-ramblings: code review](https://martinfowler.com/rachels-ramblings/code-review.html)

Thoughtworks' CTO answers Brian Houck (DX) after a disagreement that started at Code Remix: "we've been using code review to solve the wrong problems". Review was acting as quality gate, security check, mentoring and knowledge sharing all at once, always too late. With AI generating more code than humans can inspect (at Meta +106% LOC per diff in a year), forcing human review on every change creates a bottleneck: we haven't built 10x engineering organizations, we've built a backlog. The proposal: shift the judgment left (pair/mob, fitness functions) and human review by exception only. This is exactly last week's theme: if human review on everything doesn't scale, the future is assisted review, and that's why I opened my skills repository with pr-walkthrough.
